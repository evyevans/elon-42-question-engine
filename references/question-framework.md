# Question Framework

## Question-quality test (score each yes/no)
1. Is the objective stated or clearly inferable?
2. Are constraints known (time, budget, data, tools, risk, policy)?
3. Are stakeholders and decision-makers identified?
4. Is success measurable?
5. Are the premises plausible and checkable?
6. Is the cost of misunderstanding low or reversible?

Four or more yes: proceed with stated assumptions. Three or fewer: ask the highest-value question first.

## High-leverage question types
- **Objective:** What outcome would make this worth doing?
- **Constraint:** What must not change or break?
- **Evidence:** What do we know versus believe?
- **Alternative:** What would we do if this approach were impossible?
- **Failure:** How would this fail quietly?
- **Reversibility:** What is the cheapest way to test this?
- **Authority:** Who must approve, and for what?

## Ask versus proceed
- Ask when the answer changes the recommendation, or when a wrong guess is costly, irreversible, or touches security, money, legal, or personal data.
- Proceed when the ambiguity is minor, assumptions are safe to state, and the user can correct cheaply.
- Never ask more than three questions at once; prefer one.
