Drafts only. No external posts or messages have been sent. Repository: https://github.com/evyevans/elon-frame. The launch steps below are proposals, not completed activities or achieved traction.

## Category and audience

**Category statement:** Elon Frame is an open-source question-discovery skill: a portable method for identifying the question worth resolving before committing to an answer, plan or build.

The core applies to business, research, careers, education, product exploration and technical work. Its initial distribution wedge is consultants and internal AI-adoption managers facilitating discovery with product and operations teams. They have recurring opportunities to translate an uncertain concern into a shared question and a practical next step. That is a launch hypothesis, not evidence that these groups will buy.

Lead with a recognizable moment: someone proposes a solution before the team has agreed on the underlying problem. Show how a short inquiry can produce a useful artifact: the original request, a candidate question, evidence and gaps, assumptions, and the next test. Preserve the original framing when it holds up. Clear, low-risk requests should receive direct answers.

## GitHub launch copy

**Suggested release/discussion title:** Elon Frame: an open-source skill for finding the question before the answer

Many AI tasks start with a proposed solution: build a bot, choose a tool, write a strategy. Elon Frame adds a reusable inquiry method for moments when the underlying objective or evidence is uncertain.

It distinguishes the request from an inferred objective, examines assumptions, follows the pivotal unknown and offers a candidate question the user can correct. The result can be a concise question-and-evidence brief with a next test. It also accepts that no reframe may be needed.

The skill is general-purpose. The examples span business, research, careers and technical design. Consultants and people guiding team AI adoption are especially useful early evaluation partners because they see repeated discovery problems.

Try it on a real task you can share safely. Compare it with your usual assistant and its built-in clarification or planning features. Tell us where the framing helped, where it added friction and where it should have answered directly.

Source and installation instructions: https://github.com/evyevans/elon-frame

MIT licensed. The complete core is free. This is an independent project, and the examples are illustrations rather than evidence of measured results.

## Demo script: approximately 90 seconds

**0–15 seconds — the problem.**

“AI can help us answer quickly. Sometimes we need to check what we’re asking. Elon Frame is an open-source question-discovery skill. Here are two illustrative examples and one control.”

**15–40 seconds — business example.**

Show: “Build an AI follow-up bot.”

“The pivotal question is: what evidence suggests missed follow-up causes the loss? Suppose we only know that sales fell. The candidate question becomes: which recoverable leads are being lost, why, and what intervention should we test? The brief marks the cause as unknown. The next step is to inspect a small set of lost leads before choosing a solution.”

**40–65 seconds — nontechnical example.**

Show: “Help me choose a course for a career change.”

“Before listing courses, ask what work the person wants to try and what evidence they have that a credential is the missing step. A useful next question might be: what small experience would test this career direction before enrolling? Keep constraints visible; don’t assume everyone can afford unpaid exploration.”

**65–80 seconds — unchanged-frame control.**

Show: “Convert these confirmed meeting times into a chronological agenda.”

“The objective and inputs are clear. Produce the agenda. No discovery ritual and no invented hidden problem.”

**80–90 seconds — invitation.**

“Try your own task. Compare the result with your usual assistant. Does the question-and-evidence brief make the next move clearer?”

Production note: label these as illustrative scenarios. Record actual outputs, including corrections and uncertainty. Do not imply scripted examples are live user evidence.

## DM reply drafts

**“How would this work in an enterprise?”**

A useful pilot would compare it with your existing clarification workflow and assess the question, evidence gaps, time and next action. The package does not provide a hosted enterprise platform, audit system, access controls or guaranteed privacy. Those depend on your chosen tools and organizational setup.”

**“Why open source? Is there a paid version?”**

“The complete core is free under the MIT license. You can inspect, adapt and redistribute it while preserving the required notices. You still need a compatible AI tool; that provider may charge separately. Optional professional services could cover workshops, training or workflow adaptation if there is demand. Any future premium hosted service would be a separate product with its own real infrastructure and terms. Using the core doesn’t require buying those services.”

## Alternatives and the differentiation hypothesis

Users can already ask an assistant to interview them, challenge assumptions or make a plan. Claude documents user interviewing and specifications; ChatGPT deep research offers clarification and editable plans. Human facilitators, discovery interviews and established framing frameworks are also credible alternatives.

The hypothesis is that a portable, inspectable method producing a question-and-evidence artifact is worth adopting across domains and tools. Test it against those substitutes. Do not claim to be first, unique, leading, or proven to prevent project failure.

## Proposed launch and learning plan

1. **Prepare:** Verify installation and links; publish the complete source, limitations, contribution route and illustrative demo. Distinguish package checks from model-behavior evaluation.
2. **Recruit:** Invite a small, explicitly described cohort of consultants and internal adoption managers through authorized channels. No automated outreach or posting is implied by this document.
3. **Compare:** Use consented, deidentified real tasks alongside ordinary assistant use and vendor interview/planning features. Hold context and time budget reasonably constant. Include nontechnical tasks and tasks needing no reframe.
4. **Learn:** Review question relevance, visible assumptions, evidence gaps, next-action clarity, actual time and rework. Record friction and cases where the original question was better. Small convenience pilots provide product learning, not population-wide efficacy estimates.
5. **Share:** Publish examples only with explicit permission, labeling edits, illustrative material and measured results separately. Ask whether people voluntarily reuse the skill, share a useful artifact or request facilitation.
6. **Decide:** Use repeated use and actual paid-service commitments to guide investment. Repository stars, impressions and downloads are distribution signals; they do not prove activation, retention, ROI or virality.

Do not forecast conversion or revenue without observations. Keep the free core complete while testing whether professional delivery solves an additional problem people will pay for.
