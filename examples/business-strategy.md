# Business strategy: growth versus a chosen outcome

**Transferable behavior:** Test a proposed means against the stated outcome, scarce resources, and opportunity cost. The setting illustrates this rule; it does not define the skill's scope.

**Request:** “We should enter three new markets to grow faster. Write the expansion plan.”

**Premature answer:** A polished market-entry plan that assumes expansion is the most useful way to improve the business.

**Pivotal question:** “Which outcome should growth improve, and what evidence makes these markets better than deepening the current one?”

**Illustrative continuation:** The founder reports a profit target and limited management capacity. A candidate question is: “Which use of our capacity is most likely to improve sustainable profit over the next year?” This is offered for correction, not substituted as the founder's true motive.

**Options:** Deepen the current segment, run one bounded market test, expand broadly, or defer while resolving an evidence gap. Compare contribution economics, demand evidence, execution capacity and opportunity cost. Do not fabricate market size or margins.

**Test:** Seek actual customer demand and realistic cost data for one promising segment, with a decision threshold chosen by the owner. If evidence already strongly favors the requested expansion and capacity exists, preserve the original plan and execute it within scope.

**Evaluation:** Makes the objective and scarce resource visible, tests the premise without rejecting ambition, and distinguishes a strategy hypothesis from validated demand.
