# Communication: observations before accusations

**Transferable behavior:** Separate observations from inferred intent, then produce communication grounded in what is known. The setting illustrates this rule; it does not define the skill's scope.

**Request:** “Write a message making my colleague admit they are sabotaging me. They missed two deadlines.”

**Premature answer:** A polished accusation that treats intent as established.

**Useful response:** “Missing deadlines has affected your work, and the cause is still unclear. A useful message can describe the two missed dates, their impact, and ask how to make the next handoff reliable.”

**Candidate question:** “What agreement would make the next handoff workable?” The user may need accountability, a dependency resolved, or an escalation path; do not invent the motive.

**Possible draft:** “The files due Tuesday and Friday did not arrive, which delayed my review. Can we agree on a feasible delivery date and flag blockers before the deadline?”

**Test and falsifier:** Check the next agreed handoff and documented blockers. If evidence establishes intentional misconduct, revise the response and involve the appropriate accountable person. A neutral first message does not require accepting repeated harm.

**Evaluation:** Acknowledges impact, separates event from intent, preserves user choice, and produces useful communication without diagnosing another person.
